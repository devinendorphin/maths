# Established foundations and work still to compare

Minimum-cost flow, node potentials, reduced costs and residual-network optimality are established. The primary implementation consulted here is official NetworkX 3.4.2 [network_simplex](https://github.com/networkx/networkx/blob/networkx-3.4.2/networkx/algorithms/flow/networksimplex.py), especially `reduced_cost`, entering-edge selection and the nonnegative-reduced-cost termination argument. Its source was retrieved over verified HTTPS on 9 October 2026 and is recorded in Source-receipt.json. The potential sign convention in this project's derivation is the negative of that implementation's convention; the underlying condition is equivalent.

That source credits Z. Király and P. Kovács, *Efficient implementation of minimum-cost flow algorithms*, Acta Universitatis Sapientiae, Informatica 4(1):67–118 (2012), and Barr, Glover and Klingman, *Enhancement of spanning tree labeling procedures for network optimization*, INFOR 17(1):16–34 (1979). Those are identified primary references, not papers whose full text was reviewed in this turn. The implementation is an authoritative inspected baseline; a broader originality assessment still requires reading primary dynamic minimum-cost-flow, sensitivity and fair-allocation work.

The prior repository [novelty audit](../../research-notes/Novelty-audit-2026-10-08.md) and [121–130 assessment](../../research121/Novelty.md) already credit affine interpolation/scaling, kinetic certificates, CP 2024 proof-producing DP, VIPR and exact-SCIP logging/completion. These established ingredients do not become new by being applied to allocation.

Potential contributions must be specific: a proved bound under a declared update/budget model, a certificate format with a defined assurance or size advantage, or replicated complete-cost evidence against compatible methods. The checked example and project charter are useful problem formulation, not evidence of such a contribution or literature priority.
