# Curiosity, credit and participation

This project welcomes questions from amateurs, practitioners and mathematicians. A person's background is not a substitute for checking a claim, and a claim's failure is not a judgment about the person's worth. A promising question can survive a reduction in the scope of its original claims.

Human contributors set questions, supply context, choose values, interpret consequences and review the reasoning. Software and language models can assist with derivation, coding and exploration; their outputs remain attributable, inspectable and corrigible. Credit prior mathematics and each actual contribution. Preserve failed attempts and disagreements that change what the evidence supports.

Invite collaboration through reproducible public artifacts; do not speak for absent mathematicians, assign them work, or declare that a field has been completed. Do not publish anyone's personal story, health information or identity without their consent. The mathematical project can honor intellectual dignity without making claims about private experiences.

For actual allocation applications, affected people must help define needs, eligibility, commitments, tradeoffs and appeals. Data and model limitations should be intelligible to them. A checked optimum is a statement about a stated model, not permission to deploy a decision over people.
