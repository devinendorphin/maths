# Findings from experiments 121–130

Safe reuse worked on the frozen models, and its cost depended on how many questions were asked and which proof/checker deployment supplied the facts. A separately trained policy selected point certification for sparse streams, snapshots for dense streams and a four-entry point cache for repeated streams, with a mathematical positive-factor override. Wrong density forecasts preserved correctness but could choose an expensive route. These are small finite comparisons, not general speed or scaling claims.

Seven base models were used: two training models, four models excluded from fitting, and a three-item crossing control. The main campaign produced 451 records. Six separately frozen runs exercised a binding preparation limit; sixty further runs included the selector inside the timing boundary. The 72 training records remain separate. Six model-change variants are identified as mutation controls rather than independently sampled transfer models. All seeds, method definitions and limits were fixed before headline execution; eval8 had disclosed API-gate exposure before the source freeze.

## Descriptive costs

Times below are primary-directory medians of complete parent plus required waited-child CPU seconds over three repetitions. All replica medians, ranges, phases and differences remain in Summary.json. They are not statistical significance estimates. Method boundaries include proof construction/checking, required completion, admission, curve discovery, I/O, failed probes and maintenance. Common logging, SDK installation and independent audits are separate.

For twenty mixed VeriPB requests, direct processes took 2.676 CPU seconds and isolated fork children 1.025. This deployment reduces repeated imports; it does not weaken the checking rules or carry checked proof state across children.

| Three certified observations | Native/VIPR | CP/direct processes | CP/isolated fork | Exact SCIP plus completion/checking |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| eval8 | 0.0096 | 0.3996 | 0.2501 | 0.0581 |
| eval10 | 0.0129 | 0.4819 | 0.2837 | 0.1006 |
| eval12 | 0.0231 | 0.5690 | 0.3730 | 0.1113 |
| factor12 | 0.0201 | 0.5552 | 0.3865 | 0.1056 |

Native certification is cheaper on these small fixtures in these deployments. This is not a general conclusion about DP: CP uses the recovered ≤16-item proof-producing adaptation, external process proof generation and a specifically identified VeriPB deployment. The full-cost proof formats, encoded auxiliary states and startup costs differ. Exact SCIP is a real native certificate comparison, with completed and raw identities retained separately.

On factor12, the dense CP/fork factor route takes 0.214 CPU seconds versus 0.298 for CP/fork snapshot construction; native snapshot is 0.016. Those numbers distinguish an admission shortcut within one producer from the producer/checker cost itself. On eval12, native dense points take about 0.100 CPU seconds in the density experiment, guarded reuse 0.037 and immutable reuse 0.035. Sparse single observations favor points. Small guarded/immutable timing reversals are retained rather than treated as a theorem about their relative speed.

The selector-inclusive dense eval12 policy takes 0.035 CPU seconds. In the forecast control its correct dense choice takes 0.034 versus 0.107 for the wrong sparse forecast, which chooses points. Sparse correct/wrong costs are 0.020/0.034. The frozen policy is not always the cheapest measured route: equivalent sparse paths differ slightly and the eval10 repeated local window can compete with LRU4. Training took 1.999 wall seconds; its summed measured route CPU was 2.043 seconds. That CPU sum excludes fitting/report bookkeeping, while the training wall boundary includes fitting and saving. These setup costs are disclosed separately; no training amortization threshold is inferred.

The binding cap preserves correctness but can lose density savings: eval12 dense capped preparation plus fallback has a primary median about 0.110 CPU seconds. The failed preparation is charged even though some already produced point facts can be used in fallback. Policy decisions, counts and proof identities are independent of runtime differences.

## Audit, replication and retained discrepancies

The main independent auditor checks 8,626 answer records and 3,543 original-model proof bindings, replays 147 distinct accepted proofs (95 native, 42 CP, 10 SCIP) and rejects a deliberately false objective bound for every distinct proof. The selector supplement checks another 852 answers and 210 bindings, replaying 32 native proofs with false-bound controls; these are a subset of the main proof set, not 32 additional distinct proofs. Repeated answer checks are not distinct inputs.

The first auditor expected 551 records, an arithmetic error; the frozen stage counts sum to 451. It exited before answer checking. The second auditor's last-row edit was accepted for one SCIP certificate because the checker had already obtained sufficient bounds and stopped before reading unused trailing derivations. This was an ineffective false-optimum control, not evidence of a false optimum being certified. Its altered artifact is retained. The corrected control changes the asserted RTP objective range to a strictly false bound and is rejected for every distinct proof. The original auditors, freezes and amendments remain visible.

Fresh-directory execution repeats all 451 main, six cap and sixty selector-inclusive records. Every logical field is compared, including mathematical answers, packings, model scopes, raw/final proof and formula hashes, endpoint rules, policy choices, refresh decisions and budget/eviction traces. Only measurement fields are excluded from exact equality, with reasons recorded in Replication.json; timing and memory differences remain in Summary.json. The external SDK and original frozen training evidence/proofs are shared. This is not independent installation or a new training replication.

The earlier published 273-worker batch, recovered historical 300-worker batch and provisional 111–120 continuation remain separate evidence sets. This campaign adds new compatible comparisons rather than assuming their results agree. Established mathematical facts and proof-producing DP are credited in Novelty.md; potential implementation and empirical contributions are described without a blanket originality ban or a first-priority claim.
