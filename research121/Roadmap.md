# Experiments 121–130: compatible proof production and maintenance

The inquiry remains when an exact answer stays optimal, when admitted evidence applies, and when complete checked reuse costs less than fresh solving/certification. The recovered official CP 2024 adaptation is now a usable baseline. This campaign does not repeat evidence recovery or infer agreement between the different earlier 101–110 batches.

| Experiment | Frozen question and comparison | Acceptance and limits |
|---|---|---|
| 121 | Can a pristine imported VeriPB parent amortize startup while each proof is checked in a separate fresh fork? Compare direct processes and fork children on identical alternating valid/false/malformed requests. | All decisions must match independently repeated checking; parent never checks a proof; child CPU/IPC/startup charged. Deployment amortization, not logical incremental checking. |
| 122 | What does full point certification cost with adapted CP/process, CP/fork, native/VIPR and actual exact SCIP certificates on matched new fixtures? | Original model/objective binding, exact subset oracle, external checks and false bounds; SCIP completion charged. No ordinary-SCIP duplicate certificate presented as a native SCIP proof. |
| 123 | Can CP or native endpoint certificates support the same admitted intervals, and how do those routes compare with positive-factor reuse? | Full curve discovery, endpoint construction/checking, admission and queries charged; distinguish point proof format from the separate interval implication. |
| 124 | How does query count affect guarded and immutable reuse versus native point certification? | One/three/seven/thirteen observations on two held-out nonfactor fixtures; complete totals, no universal crossover claim. |
| 125 | Do rational observations at crossings, ties, and interval edges remain exact under CP/native proofs and snapshots? | All internal discovered crossings ±1/1000 and ties, both edges, explicit outside-edge rejection; cap ten internal crossings and source input range. | 126 | Does a policy trained separately discriminate sparse, dense and repeated workloads and transfer to held-out models? | Train two separate models; freeze choices before evaluation. | 127 | What are actual cache/Python/proof bytes and sampled simultaneous process-tree memory under byte limits? | Independent workers, unchanged checker exit, corrected PPid discovery, actual CP/native buffers; 4 KiB/128 KiB budgets, oversized bypass and eviction. Sampling limitations disclosed. |
| 128 | Do live CP/native fact caches reject or recertify changed model and checker deployments? | Capacity/weight/affine-objective changes recertify; continuous/nonlinear models reject; actual process/fork or binary deployment epoch change evicts. No arbitrary unannounced-code monitoring claim. |
| 129 | What happens when external proof copies are edited after immutable admission or during explicit refresh? | Captured immutable facts stay fixed; hash/refreshed malformed/false evidence rejects; checked fallback costs included. CP formula/proof and native proof routes kept distinct. |
| 130 | How robust is the frozen policy to wrong density forecasts and capped preparation? | Matched sparse/dense streams; points, correct/wrong forecast policy, four-certificate snapshot cap with charged failed preparation and exact point fallback. |

New held-out models have 8, 10, 12 and 12 items; only the last is a positive-factor fixture. Training has 8 and 10 items. The crossing control has three. Signed integer affine profits, positive integer weights and fixed capacity are unchanged; rational queries use exact arithmetic. Model counts are seven, not the number of repeated workers.

The CP source/patch/binary is the recovered ≤16-item adaptation, unchanged here. These comparisons are new measurements within those named deployments.
