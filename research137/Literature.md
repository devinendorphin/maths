# Established ingredients and what was compared

The [official NetworkX 3.4.2 documentation](https://networkx.org/documentation/networkx-3.4.2/reference/algorithms/generated/networkx.algorithms.flow.network_simplex.html)
identifies its primal network-simplex solver, exact integer-data use and the
Király/Kovács (2012) and Barr/Glover/Klingman (1979) algorithm lineage. This
continuation installs that pinned release; it is the cold optimizer for every
route. Its installed module SHA-256 is recorded in the deployment freeze and
the full installed source is archived. Completion potentials are produced by
the unchanged predecessor's residual shortest-distance implementation.

[OR-Tools' official min-cost-flow introduction](https://developers.google.com/optimization/flow/mincostflow)
provides the established balances/capacity/cost formulation. The predecessor's
versioned min_cost_flow.h source inspection records that incremental/warm methods
already exist in GenericMinCostFlow. OR-Tools is not installed or compared here.
Savings against cold NetworkX and full residual rechecking do not establish
superiority over specialist incremental solvers.

The conditional residual-sign argument is inherited established sensitivity
mathematics. The new implementation removes full hashes/copies from accepted
noncompacting patches and measures a stated persistent-evidence interface.
Hash chains, version checks, ownership, checkpoint compaction and atomic rename
are also established mechanisms. Full dynamic-flow, persistent-certificate and
checkpoint-priority literature has not been exhaustively reviewed. No first,
new general theorem, cryptographic security or policy/fairness claim is made.

Documentation was consulted through public primary sources during this turn.
It supports attribution; it supplies no independent evidence for the new timing
measurements. Those are supported by the frozen raw sessions and paired audits.
