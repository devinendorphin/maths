## Findings now supported and compatible continuation

The historical comparison is now supported by recovered source, artifacts and successful proof checks. Its recorded small-instance process-per-proof CP point route cost 25.7–28.6 times the native route's complete median CPU, with 13.8–28.0 times the proof bytes. The original report attributes roughly 97–98% of the CP route's CPU to external children. Those are historical measurements in its particular adapted deployment, not timings remeasured here and not evidence that DP is generally inferior. Software development/deployment is recorded separately from warm per-worker construction, I/O, solution expansion and checking. Preserve that accounting distinction when defining any future amortized comparison.

The original endpoint-snapshot, guarded/factor, SCIP reoptimization, local-window, cache and zero-node interruption comparisons are now available under their actual experiment numbers. [Alignment.md](Alignment.md) includes their recovered mapping. They add evidence for the trajectory; they do not replace the different questions tested by the 273-worker batch or the later eighteen/twenty-item continuation.

The later exact-SCIP work answers a gap left by this recovered campaign: nine actual native SCIP certificate observations, including required completion. The fork adapter answers a VIPR startup-deployment question, not VeriPB amortization. The density-policy and unmodified process-tree peak-memory limits remain open. Recovering the CP comparison does not remove those limits or formally verify Python admission/cache code.

The previously proposed step “first obtain the authors' implementation and reproduce the missing baseline” is now redundant for evidence recovery. It would need its own frozen protocol: the recovered adapter admits at most sixteen items, so applying it to eighteen/twenty-item continuation fixtures requires an explicit new adaptation and validation. No such new performance campaign is claimed here, and no valid earlier run was repeated merely to repair numbering.
