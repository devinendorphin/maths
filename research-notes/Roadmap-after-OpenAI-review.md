# Proposed experiments 96–100 after the OpenAI review

Proposed 8 October 2026, following the [sourced review](OpenAI-mathematics-review-2026-10-08.md). These are research proposals, not frozen protocols or completed results. Experiments 86–95 remain the latest completed batch.

The question is whether exact interval proofs can reduce the total work of repeatedly certifying a changing optimization problem while preserving a clear checking contract. Keep fixed integer weights and capacity, signed affine profits, exact arithmetic, and bounded construction. New model classes can wait until this bridge has stronger support.

| Experiment | Concrete task | Evidence required | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 96: Claim and dependency scope | Map the active proof path from original model to point certificate, endpoint connector, receipt admission, and reuse. Give each claim its assumptions, verifier and dependencies. Check same-model, capacity-change, and checker-version cases. | A small claim ledger and dependency graph; actual accept/reject outcomes with valid and invalid controls. A hash seal alone is not sufficient evidence. | Which obligation is presently unproved or dependent on trusted code? |
| 97: Formal endpoint connector | Independently specify and Lean-check the affine endpoint regret theorem and its zero-gap optimality corollary. Connect rational endpoint objectives to those definitions. | Pinned Lean/mathlib versions, accepted theorem and axiom list, statement comparison, and explicit coverage limits. Include a concrete nonlinear counterexample and a changed-feasible-set counterexample to explain excluded hypotheses. | Can a small formal component close the mathematical bridge without claiming the solver or cache is formally verified? |
| 98: Compact exact witnesses | Compare the current adapter with an established proof-logging route for a small matched signed-affine problem set. Start with CP 2024 certifying-DP and VIPR/VeriPB expressiveness rather than a generic solver rewrite. | Checked original-problem binding, complete coverage, proof steps and bytes, construction and checker cost, and an explicit unsupported status if interval reasoning cannot be represented. | Does our format add a real capability or measurable economy over close prior work? |
| 99: Budgeted progress and invalidation | Interrupt proof construction at declared budgets; preserve only sound partial information. Exercise model, theorem-rule and checker changes, and resumption through compatible recorded states. | Separate optimal, bounded-gap, incomplete and invalid statuses. Reject stale admissions; charge attempted construction and fallback. A missing witness must never be reported as a false theorem. | Can useful progress survive a cap without overstating completion or retaining an inapplicable proof? |
| 100: Full-cost comparison | Compare any successful new path against independent checked point solves, the current guarded interval method, and the positive-factor shortcut where applicable. Include matched dense and sparse observations. | Fresh logical checks, full preparation/admission/reuse/checker CPU, proof size, failures and declared input scope. Report where reuse loses as well as wins. | Does stronger checking or a smaller certificate repay its cost on a defined class? |

Before execution, freeze modest inputs, resource caps, hypotheses, accounting boundaries and stop conditions. Set a bounded capability gate for Lean and adapter availability; if a dependency cannot be obtained within the cap, report that limitation instead of substituting unchecked text for a formal proof. The review environment currently has no Lean/Lake installation, and this proposal does not claim one was run.

Reuse the existing sources and small fixtures when appropriate. Additional cases should expose a new assumption or distinguish two methods. Repeating a broad threshold sweep would add little evidence for these questions.

Only use completed components in experiment 100. If experiment 98 establishes that an adapter cannot express the required interval obligations economically, preserve that negative result and compare the existing methods; do not silently weaken the proof contract. If the new route has no practical benefit, stop expanding it.

Keep documentation and essential checked witnesses compact in Git. Store bulky search diagnostics using the established archive policy. Installing a proof assistant or constructing a certificate belongs to its own recorded work budget; benchmark timing should separately identify deployment setup, mathematical checking, and per-query computation.

The target contribution is a specific supported capability or cost advantage. An elementary lemma, an inherited novelty caveat, or a large count of cases cannot substitute for that comparison.
