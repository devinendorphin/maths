# Experiments 86–95: what can safely be reused, and what it costs

All ten experiments completed on 60 frozen input records: 101 headline workers and 147 timing workers, totaling **248**. Every recorded mathematical audit passed: **20,324 integer checks, 22,420 numerical checks and 3,837 bound/control checks**. Integer checks are included in the numerical checks in some paths; these totals count operations across workers, not distinct independent observations. No worker reached a limit. The original run took 43.01 wall seconds within its 900-second limit; that excludes implementation, the subsequent re-audit, replication and archival work.

The independent full re-audit reproduced the mathematical counts. Official VIPR accepted all **466 unique proof files** and rejected all **466 deliberately lowered final bounds**. A fresh 248-worker execution matched every status and logical signature. Original timings remain preserved; the replica is not pooled into them. Detailed evidence and pinned dependencies are preserved in one small repository archive as a fallback after two stalled Drive uploads. The source/results directory keeps compact review material; Archive-storage.json records the storage status.

## The ten findings

**86 — Exact endpoint reasoning works within its contract.** Six inputs produced 23 positive-length real intervals. Independent checks covered their endpoints, rational interior points and integer observations, including ties and crossings. A single packing attaining verified bounds at both endpoints is optimal throughout under fixed feasibility and affine objectives. The connector rejects nonlinear models, changing feasibility and outside-domain use. The algebraic argument, rather than a finite sample alone, justifies the interval guarantee.

**87 — Endpoint gaps give a controlled error bound.** Six candidates passed the interpolated additive-regret check. A stable control had zero gap; other declared endpoint gaps reached 117. A midpoint optimum can be poor at an endpoint. Its worst allowed gap is the larger endpoint gap; interior correctness cannot be inferred without checking those gaps. This batch uses exact endpoint optima, not an approximate production solver.

**88 — Restricting feasibility can preserve a checked optimum.** Twenty-six transfers were accepted when the new capacity was no larger and the old optimal packing still fit. Forty-eight transfers were rejected for expansion or infeasibility. Rejection does not itself prove that every rejected packing is wrong; it means this transfer theorem does not apply. The dedicated fixed-time subset connector is separate from the conservative exact-model cache.

**89 — Nonlinear change breaks the naive endpoint rule.** A capacity-one candidate valued at 1 beats a competitor `8t−4t²` at both endpoints of [0,2], yet loses by 3 at t=1. The curvature correction bounds that loss by 4. Raising the candidate to 10 supplies enough endpoint margin to certify exact reuse throughout. That sufficient margin criterion certified one of six inputs; all computed bounds passed independent optimum and analytic-extremum checks. This is an explicit limit/control for affine reuse, not a general nonlinear optimizer.

**90 — A particular proof can fail while the answer stays right.** All 13 scalar covers were independently checked for complete disjoint coverage and repriced against every feasible packing. Four positive-scaling inputs and the constructed control had certificate failure at time 1 with no future strict answer loss. Two non-scaling inputs had failure at 1 and strict loss at 2 or 3; two had simultaneous failure/loss. Four zero-slope controls had neither failure nor loss. The fixed scalar price causes the scaling failures; scaling the proof itself avoids them. These results do not establish an inherent need to renew every possible proof.

**91 — Reuse repaid preparation on these dense streams.** Over four declared inputs, the median summed parent-plus-checker-child CPU was 0.750380 seconds for full independent point proofs, 0.122832 for envelope reuse and 0.118488 for guarded reuse. The latter two small totals do not establish that integrity checking is free or faster; timing variation can reverse their ordering. All preparation, native proof generation, file writing, required VIPR checking and reuse validation were charged. Both envelope methods beat points on all four per-input medians.

**92 — Sparse requests can reverse the result.** The same seed-1 problem requested 33, 5 or 2 observations. Median costs were respectively 0.192053/0.026752/0.013070 seconds for independent points and 0.052502/0.050503/0.043757 for guarded envelopes. Thus guarded reuse won the dense stream but lost both sparse streams. Its full envelope preparation was charged even for endpoint-only requests. Seed 0 supplied matching headline controls without repeated timing; the three timed variants are one underlying problem, not three independent draws.

**93 — Receipt consistency checks rejected the declared corruptions.** Two inputs rejected thirteen mutations each, including altered proof bytes, metadata and its recomputed seal, wrong checker identity, unsupported model scope and invalid/suboptimal packings. Matching the canonical original-problem prefix catches forged model metadata even when its checksum is recomputed. The seal is an unsalted checksum, not a signature; trusted admission, a fixed checker and truthful scope remain assumptions. Concurrency and compromised-process security are outside this test.

**94 — Model epochs prevent unsafe stale reuse.** Four sequences contained two observations of an original model and two of a changed model. Cold paths generated sixteen proofs; guarded caching generated eight with eight valid hits. Every changed optimum exceeded the old bound, including a capacity-expansion control: its optimum rose from 4 to 7. Reusing the old bound would have been mathematically false. Exact repeated model/time contexts retained their own valid proofs.

**95 — A known structural shortcut transferred where its assumptions held.** Seven fresh inputs included dense random, scaling and zero-slope streams, plus a sign-reversal control. Positive common-factor recognition used one checked base bound on four applicable inputs. Two random inputs and the sign-reversal input used the guarded envelope fallback. The factor method had median aggregate CPU 0.227311 seconds versus 4.658316 for repeated point proofs and 0.264747 for guarded envelopes. It beat points on all seven per-input medians, but beat guarded envelopes on five of seven. Comparing only the four applicable cases is more informative than attributing fallback timing differences to the shortcut. Positive scaling invariance is established mathematics.

## What this adds to the inquiry

[Mathematical-guarantees.md](Mathematical-guarantees.md) states conditional interval, gap, subset, curvature and expiry arguments, and the exact preparation-versus-reuse cost condition. Known mathematical ingredients support a useful implementation and original finite-sample findings. Their combination's originality is separately assessed in the [novelty audit](../research-notes/Novelty-audit-2026-10-08.md). A broad “no novelty claim” must not be interpreted as a prohibition on recognizing a supported contribution.

This batch makes the distinction between answer stability, certificate applicability and total verification cost explicit. It favors dense-stream reuse on these small inputs and reveals a matched sparse-stream failure. It does not prove universal speedup, new arithmetic, production superiority, a competitive bound for arbitrary renewal costs or recovery of Brooks's unpublished mathematics.

The small exact kernels, native partition certificate, VIPR adapter and compiled checker snapshot remain declared dependencies. Inputs have at most twelve items and small capacities. Timing subsets contain four inputs in 91, one matched problem in 92 and seven dependent-profile records in 95, each with three sequential rotated repeats. CPU is work across parent and checker processes, not latency; peak RSS was not measured. No statistical confidence or formal verification is inferred from these tests. Freeze, protocol, inputs, findings, full-audit and fresh-replication receipts make the scope reviewable.
